# T56. Restore sticky/floating blog post TOC after T55

## Goal

Grzesiek's feedback after the T55 merge (#70, squash `e23c9c1`) on the blog post layout: the table of contents in the left column no longer floats with the article while scrolling. It stays at the top of the page.

Reference post: https://zawlodzki-web.zawlodzki.workers.dev/blog/automatyzacja-sprzedazy. Staging may not yet have T55 deployed — inspect and fix the code on current `main` (post-T55). The same mechanism should work on every blog post, not only this one. Do not add per-post hacks.

Standing prefs: no cyan anywhere; Atlas Blue `#3a48cc` for accents. Do not change CTA copy.

## Current code (inspect 2026-11-12, post-T55 on main)

- Layout lives in `PostView.astro`. After T55 the shell is 86rem, side tracks are 10rem / 14rem, the article is capped at 72ch, and desktop article type scales 18–20px. At 1440 the reading column is 896px / ~68.9ch; at 1280 it is 802px / ~66.9ch. Keep those widths.
- Two columns (TOC + content) start at `min-width: 56rem`. Three columns (TOC / content / right CTA) start at `min-width: 80rem`. Below 80rem the sidebar CTA is `display: none` and related posts sit under the article. Keep that responsive order: the right column disappears first, then the TOC.
- T55 removed `max-block-size`, `overflow-y: auto` and overscroll containment from `.sticky`, so side panels no longer have an inner scrollbar. Sticky is still applied from 56rem: `.sticky { position: sticky; inset-block-start: 7rem; }`.
- T55 also added a ResizeObserver that sets `data-page-scroll` when `panel.offsetHeight > innerHeight - offset - 16`, and `.sticky[data-page-scroll] { position: static; }`. That is the likely cause: a TOC taller than the remaining viewport is forced to `position: static` and therefore stays at the top of the page instead of floating while the article scrolls. Overflow other than `visible` or `align-items: stretch` on an ancestor can also break `position: sticky` — check both.
- Related posts stay inlined in `PostView.astro` (`[data-related-posts]`), inert until the article midpoint crosses the viewport midpoint at `min-width: 80rem`, then animate with a 240ms ease-out on `opacity` and `transform` only. Keep that behaviour.
- Mobile TOC, single mobile CTA, and share brand icons were delivered in T48 and must stay.

## Requirements

1. **Restore a sticky/floating TOC.** At every desktop width where the left-column TOC is shown (from 56rem up, including 1440, 1280 and 1100), the TOC must remain visible while the reader scrolls the article: mid-scroll and near the end, not only at the top of the page. Do not force the TOC to `position: static` just because it is taller than the viewport. Prefer a CSS-first fix; change the ResizeObserver if it is what is disabling sticky on the TOC.
2. **Keep T55 gains.** Do not regress: the wider reading column (~67–69ch at 1440 / 1280, article still capped around 72ch); title/hero alignment with the TOC; no inner scrollbar in the right column (`overflow: auto` / `overflow-y: auto` or a `max-height` / `max-block-size` that creates one). The right column may still leave the viewport with the page when it is taller than the remaining space — that behaviour must not come back on the TOC. Breakpoint order stays: right column disappears first, then TOC (3-col from 80rem, 2-col from 56rem). Related posts still reveal at about half the article with the existing motion (`transform` and `opacity` only, ~200–300ms ease-out, no layout shift; instant when `prefers-reduced-motion` is set). Mobile TOC stays collapsible and collapsed-by-default; a single CTA on mobile; share icons unchanged. CTA copy stays unchanged. Do not change global `layout.css` or tokens unless there is no other way; if that becomes necessary, add the file to `owns` in the PR and say why.
3. **Prove it in the browser.** Use verify-web (`scripts/web.sh up` on port **8791**, `wrangler dev --local`, never `astro dev`). Probe `getBoundingClientRect().top` of the TOC after scrolling to mid and late positions and show that it stays pinned near the sticky offset rather than remaining at the top of the document.
